Optical physics research · John Carroll University · 2015–2016

Biomedical optics with Prof. Peifang Tian.

Two undergraduate research projects in the JCU Physics Department, each resulting in a published abstract in the Bulletin of the American Physical Society.

Neurovascular imaging

Two-photon microscopy correction scheme

Built a simulation of two-photon laser scanning microscopy (TPLSM) to derive images of vascular structures in rodent cortices. I analyzed 3D models of specimen vasculature and their corresponding images in MATLAB, then compiled and corrected image sets to generate data justifying a correction scheme for hemodynamic imaging. Presented the work as a poster at the OSAPS conference.

  • Methods: TPLSM simulation, 3D vasculature modelling, MATLAB image analysis.
  • Outcome: data justifying an efficient correction scheme for TPLSM hemodynamic imaging.
  • Publication: Ifeanyi, S., Sauer, T., Cotton, W., Tian, P., Devor, A., Dale, A., Ruvinskaya, L., Boas, D., & Sakadžić, S. “Simulating the hemodynamic effect in imaging brain tissue using two-photon laser scanning microscopy.” Bulletin of the American Physical Society 61 (2016).
Clinical photonics

Portable spectrometer for laser tattoo removal

Assembled laboratory apparatus to measure the absorbance spectra of tattoo inks in phantom skin, then analyzed the optical spectra with self-written MATLAB scripts alongside the lab setup. I built a portable version of the spectrometer that reproduced the functionality of the lab-scale instrument — informing the wavelengths and energy parameters relevant to safe, effective treatment.

  • Methods: absorbance spectroscopy, phantom-skin samples, MATLAB spectral analysis, instrument miniaturization.
  • Outcome: a portable optical system replicating lab-scale spectrometer performance.
  • Publication: Montesano, A., Ifeanyi, S., & Tian, P. “Developing an Optical System for Laser Tattoo Removal.” Bulletin of the American Physical Society 62 (2017).
